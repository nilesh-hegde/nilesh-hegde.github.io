I am a Predoctoral Research Professional at the Fama-Miller Center for Research in Finance at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business, where I work with Ralph Koijen, George M. Constantinides, and Taisiya Sikorskaya. My research interests are in machine learning in finance and causal machine learning, with a particular focus on what learning-based methods can — and cannot — tell us about asset prices and the structure of financial markets.

I came to finance through a longer arc in algorithms and learning systems. I completed my MS in Computer Science at the Khoury College of Computer Sciences at Northeastern University, where my research moved between two areas: natural language processing and machine learning, and the economics of higher education. I worked with Carla Brodley and Felix Muzny at Northeastern's Center for Inclusive Computing (CIC), studying how computing programs grow and who they reach.

Before Northeastern, I received a BTech in Computer Science and Engineering from B.M.S. College of Engineering in Bengaluru, where my undergraduate research was in algorithmic graph theory under Suresh M. Hegde. The through-line across these stops has been the same: take ideas from algorithms and statistical learning and push them into a new domain — first graphs, then language and education, and now financial markets.
